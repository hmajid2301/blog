Original editorial illustrations for your own writing: thin outlines, generous space, and one accent colour. Inspired by the restraint of [Davide’s article graphics](https://www.davideimola.dev/blog/ai-agent-sessions-data-store), not copied from them.

**Try Illustration style, visual style, accent, and light/dark above.** Drawing treatment and blog theme are independent. The drawings inherit the selected palette. These are plain, editable SVGs—no image-generation service or illustration library.

## Four illustration treatments

- **Original linework**: fine outlines, open space, and one accent.
- **Drafting blueprint**: graph-paper structure and drafting-style joins. Try it with Nord Fieldnotes or Terminal Log.
- **Bold screen-print**: heavier strokes and square joins on a tinted surface. Try Ink & Signal for a print-like cover.
- **One-ink monochrome**: solid ink, reversed details, no coloured accent. Useful when the drawing should feel like a small book illustration.

The treatment follows you into the real article examples below. These are different renderings of the same original drawings, not newly generated subject matter.

## Watch them draw in

Each illustration draws its outlines once when it first comes into view, then brings in the accent. Use **Replay drawing** below any illustration to watch it again. There are no continuous loops or animation libraries.

## Keep the maintenance small

Each drawing is around 2–3 KB of SVG source. They share a visual vocabulary—line weight, proportions, negative space, and one accent—but each uses a different idea. Your original article and talk text is unchanged; the covers are inserted only in the demo.

The download links provide the original linework SVG source. Standalone files default to a Catppuccin Latte palette; inline versions pick up the demo’s colour variables. Blueprint, print and monochrome are CSS preview treatments, not separate exported SVG files. They stay sharp at any display size and include accessible titles and descriptions. Raster social cards would be a separate export step.

If you like the direction, keep the chosen SVG files and a tiny include—not the whole design playground. Future covers can follow the same rules, one original visual metaphor per post.
